## Why 'What' Matters

The first step to solving any problem is to understand what the problem actually is. A well‑phrased *What* question cuts through ambiguity and forces you to define the scope, the stakeholders, and the constraints.

- Clarifies objectives
- Aligns teams
- Reduces scope creep

## Distinguishing 'What' from 'Why' and 'How'

| Question | Focus | Typical Use |
|----------|-------|-------------|
| **What** | *What* is happening? | Identifying features, data, or events |
| **Why** | *Why* is it happening? | Root‑cause analysis |
| **How** | *How* do we do it? | Implementation or process design |

## Crafting Effective 'What' Questions

1. **Be Specific** – Instead of “What’s the issue?” ask “What data points are missing from the dashboard?”
2. **Use Context** – “What is the current latency of the API in production?”
3. **Avoid Leading Words** – “What are the potential bottlenecks?” not “What are the bottlenecks?”

## Real‑World Examples

- **Product Roadmap** – *What features will deliver the highest user value in Q3?*
- **DevOps** – *What is the mean time to recovery (MTTR) for the microservice?*
- **Data Science** – *What variables most strongly predict churn?*

## Takeaway

Mastering the art of asking the right *What* questions turns vague conversations into actionable plans. Practice framing, review, and iterate until the question itself becomes a clear, testable hypothesis.
